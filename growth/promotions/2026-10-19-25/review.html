Equal area does not establish equal friction

Two rectangular ducts can have the same area without establishing the same friction loss.

Illustrative clear internal dimensions: 12 x 12 in and 6 x 24 in both give 144 in^2 = 1 ft^2. At the same volume flow and reference basis, both have the same cross-sectional mean velocity. Their perimeters differ: 48 in versus 60 in.

That geometry difference matters to a friction comparison; it is not a pressure-loss ratio. Length, roughness and fittings still matter.

Lower RH does not necessarily mean less moisture

A lower relative-humidity reading does not necessarily mean moisture was removed.

In a heating-only example at fixed pressure, with no moisture added or removed, humidity ratio stays constant while relative humidity falls as temperature rises.

RH describes vapor pressure relative to saturation vapor pressure at the same temperature. Humidity ratio describes water-vapor mass per mass of dry air. Different quantities, different questions.
