# Campaign #002 — richer video directions

## Current prototype

**Code-driven technical motion graphic.** It validates the structured-content,
branding, motion, rendering, and QA pipeline. Its silent slide/card treatment is a
prototype, not the automatic final publication asset.

## Option A — The reading that cannot answer the next question

### Visual concept

A credible technician approaches an operating air handler, prepares a differential
manometer, and observes return and supply static readings. The visual stops before
claiming a universal test-port procedure. The question “So what is the CFM?”
appears, followed by the answer: the two pressure readings do not independently
establish airflow.

### Opening hook

Close shot of the manometer display and gloved hand: **“This number is not CFM.”**

### Scene sequence

1. Establish a clean mechanical-room environment and identifiable air handler.
2. Show the instrument without presenting probe placement as universal instruction.
3. Overlay the illustrative -0.20 and +0.35 in. w.c. readings.
4. Resolve the 0.55 in. w.c. magnitude, then interrupt the expected CFM answer.
5. State the equipment-data/operating-condition limitation and end on the resource.

### Required media

Licensed or owner-created technician/equipment footage or stills, manometer close-up,
Remotion overlays, captions, AnyHVAC end card, and recorded provenance.

### Narration concept

“These readings describe a pressure relationship. Their magnitude is 0.55 inches
water column—but that does not independently tell you system airflow.” Narration
adds context rather than reading the displayed values verbatim.

### Audio concept

Narration without music, retaining subtle, rights-cleared mechanical-room ambience.
Instrument and room sound remain secondary and never imply an unsafe procedure.

### Technical risks

Wrong probe placement, unsafe access, missing PPE, implying one equipment boundary
fits every system, or showing an instrument configuration as authoritative.

### Production complexity

Medium to high.

### Current tools vs future provider

Current tools can perform typography, labels, captions, compositing, and final
assembly. Authentic source footage must be owner-supplied/licensed; otherwise a
future approved visual provider is needed and the scene must be technically
reviewed and labeled illustrative where appropriate.

## Option B — Pressure field versus airflow stream

**SELECTED FOR CAMPAIGN #002 FINAL OWNER REVIEW.** The implemented version is a technically controlled 2D interpretation with original local sound design and complete on-screen explanation.

### Visual concept

A stylized cutaway duct becomes a cinematic but explicitly **CONCEPTUAL** visual.
Color fields show negative return and positive supply static relationships while
moving particles represent air motion. The two encodings separate pressure from
airflow so the viewer can see why one does not directly equal the other.

### Opening hook

Particles freeze while the pressure field remains: **“Pressure is not airflow.”**

### Scene sequence

1. Enter a simplified duct/equipment cutaway with moving airflow particles.
2. Separate the particle layer (air motion) from the color field (pressure concept).
3. Introduce the illustrative return and supply readings.
4. Calculate the 0.55 in. w.c. magnitude without assigning a CFM.
5. Recombine the layers and state that equipment data and operating conditions are
   needed to support airflow evaluation.

### Required media

Conceptual 2D/3D duct cutaway, particle/streamline animation, pressure-field layer,
labels, narration, captions, restrained sound bed, and provenance.

### Narration concept

Explain what each visual encoding represents, emphasizing that the pressure field
and moving-air quantity are related system information but not interchangeable.

### Audio concept

Narration plus a very light commercially cleared tonal bed; subtle airflow texture
may support transitions but should never mimic a calibrated measurement.

### Technical risks

Viewers may interpret particle speed, color intensity, or direction as measured
data. The visual must be labeled conceptual and avoid quantitative visual scales
that are not supported by the reference.

### Production complexity

Medium for simplified 2D; high for cinematic 3D.

### Current tools vs future provider

Remotion can produce a technically controlled 2D version. A true duct fly-through,
volumetric pressure visualization, or realistic 3D particle field requires a future
approved media-generation or 3D provider.

## Option C — Exploded air-handler pressure story

### Visual concept

A simplified air handler separates into broad functional sections so the viewer can
see that system configuration and the defined external boundary matter. The
equipment then reassembles before the readings are shown. The visual is labeled
**SIMPLIFIED** and avoids presenting one component sequence as universal.

### Opening hook

The unit separates around a highlighted boundary: **“Where you measure changes
what the pressure reading means.”**

### Scene sequence

1. Show a neutral assembled air handler.
2. Separate simplified return, filter/accessory, fan/equipment, and supply regions.
3. Highlight a non-prescriptive external boundary and note that equipment differs.
4. Reassemble and show the -0.20 / +0.35 / 0.55 arithmetic.
5. Conclude that the result does not independently establish airflow.

### Required media

Technically reviewed equipment model or illustration, separable component layers,
boundary highlight, labels, narration, light effects, end card, and provenance.

### Narration concept

Use the exploded view to explain why manufacturer procedure, equipment
configuration, and operating condition matter before interpreting a TESP result.

### Audio concept

Narration plus restrained mechanical movement effects. Optional music is minimal
and ducked beneath narration.

### Technical risks

Incorrect or overly universal component sequence, impossible clearances, inaccurate
airflow direction, or turning a conceptual boundary into a field procedure.

### Production complexity

High.

### Current tools vs future provider

Current tools can animate supplied layers, labels, and boundary highlights. A
credible exploded model or cutaway requires owner-supplied technical artwork or a
future approved provider followed by detailed technical review.

## Selection record

Option B was selected because it offers the clearest concept-to-visual match with
the lowest dependence on realistic people or equipment accuracy. The controlled
2D final is ready for owner campaign review. Selection is not publication approval.
