TESP is not airflow

https://www.anyhvac.net/resources/airflow-static-pressure-measurement

linkedin · Tue, Oct 27, 4:00 PM EDT

TESP is a pressure measurement—not a direct airflow measurement.

For an illustrative conventional negative-return/positive-supply arrangement, -0.20 and +0.35 in. w.c. give a TESP magnitude of 0.55 in. w.c. That arithmetic does not establish CFM, acceptable operation, or a design pressure allowance.

Use the exact equipment boundary, approved probe locations, operating mode and manufacturer data. There is no universal pair of probe locations for every system.

Which pressure are you comparing?

https://www.anyhvac.net/resources/airflow-static-pressure-measurement

linkedin · Thu, Oct 29, 5:00 PM EDT

Static pressure, velocity pressure, total pressure and pressure drop answer different questions.

At the same measurement point, total pressure is static pressure plus velocity pressure. A pressure drop compares pressures across a defined component or section at a stated operating condition. TESP uses the equipment-defined external boundary.

Before comparing readings, identify the quantity, reference, location, instrument and operating condition. Do not interchange the terms or infer airflow from pressure alone.

A design pressure budget, step by step

https://www.anyhvac.net/tools/air-distribution

linkedin · Fri, Oct 30, 3:00 PM EDT

Design available static pressure is a budget—not a measured TESP shortcut.

Illustrative inputs only: a valid design fan external-static allowance at the intended airflow of 0.80 in. w.g. minus 0.30 in. w.g. of applicable external component losses leaves 0.50 in. w.g. available static pressure. With 250 ft total effective length, FR = 0.50 × 100 / 250 = 0.20 in. w.g./100 ft.

Account for each applicable loss once and use a valid design allowance and TEL. These are arithmetic examples, not recommended targets, measured system performance or a compliant-design finding.
