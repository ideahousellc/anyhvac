# Campaign #002 — carousel alternative

## Concept

**Two layers, two meanings:** a six-slide visual story contrasting pressure
relationship with airflow quantity. It uses an original simplified equipment/duct
cutaway and bold visual encoding—not website cards or pasted PDF pages.

## Slide 1 — Cover

A split conceptual duct: blue pressure field on one side, airflow streamlines on
the other. Headline: **TESP is not airflow.** Small label: `CONCEPTUAL`.

## Slide 2 — The two questions

One continuous visual, divided by a centerline:

- Pressure side: “What resistance is the fan working against?”
- Airflow side: “How much air is moving?”

Use symbols and spatial contrast rather than paragraph text.

## Slide 3 — The illustrative readings

Show return and supply sides around a simplified equipment silhouette. Place
`-0.20 in. w.c.` on return and `+0.35 in. w.c.` on supply. Include: “Approved probe
locations and boundaries vary by equipment.”

## Slide 4 — The arithmetic

The two magnitudes travel into a single clean equation:
`|-0.20| + |+0.35| = 0.55 in. w.c.` Label it **illustrative arithmetic** and avoid a
website-style formula card.

## Slide 5 — The limit

The `0.55 in. w.c.` value attempts to transform into `CFM`, then visibly stops.
Takeaway: **This result does not independently establish airflow.** Supporting
line: correct equipment data and operating conditions are still required.

## Slide 6 — CTA

Zoom out to the complete conceptual system and invite the viewer to open the free
HVAC Airflow & Static Pressure Measurement Quick Reference at AnyHVAC.net. Use a
restrained branded end frame, not a screenshot of the resource page.

## Technical takeaway

Static pressure and airflow provide different system information. The illustrative
TESP magnitude does not independently determine a specific CFM.

## Production requirements

An original vector-style cutaway, reusable pressure-field and airflow-streamline
layers, AnyHVAC identity, 4:5 source layout with safe crops, source notes, and
technical review. Current local tools can create the simplified diagram and layout;
no external provider is required for an initial lightweight proof.
