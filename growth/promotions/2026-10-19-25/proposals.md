## Production text

### Geometry carousel (six slides)
1. SAME AREA. SAME FRICTION? — Equal area does not establish equal friction.
2. TWO SECTIONS. ONE AREA. — 12 × 12 in = 144 in². 6 × 24 in = 144 in². Illustrative clear internal dimensions.
3. SAME FLOW. SAME MEAN VELOCITY. — Both are 1 ft². At the same volume-flow reference basis, V = Q / A gives the same cross-sectional mean velocity.
4. THE GEOMETRY CHANGES. — Perimeter: 48 in versus 60 in. Area alone does not describe the wetted perimeter. Not a pressure-loss ratio.
5. CHECK THE FRICTION METHOD. — Rectangular friction equivalence needs more than area. Length, roughness and fittings still matter. Hydraulic diameter is not the tool’s equivalent round diameter.
6. COMPARE. THEN VERIFY. — Explore round and rectangular options in the free AnyHVAC Duct Calculator. Check actual geometry and project requirements. Example, not a duct recommendation.

### Relative-humidity carousel (six slides)
1. LOWER RH. LESS MOISTURE? — Not necessarily. Relative humidity and humidity ratio answer different questions.
2. TWO DIFFERENT MEASURES. — RH compares vapor pressure with saturation vapor pressure at that temperature. Humidity ratio is water-vapor mass per mass of dry air.
3. WARM THE SAME AIR. — Heating only. Fixed pressure. No moisture added or removed. Humidity ratio stays constant.
4. RH CAN FALL. — Saturation vapor pressure increases as temperature rises. RH falls in this heating-only example; the water-vapor content has not decreased.
5. DEHUMIDIFICATION IS DIFFERENT. — Removing moisture lowers humidity ratio. A lower RH reading alone does not prove moisture removal. This is a concept, not equipment diagnosis.
6. CHECK THE FULL AIR STATE. — Use the free Psychrometric Calculator. Set matching inputs and pressure; compare temperature, RH and humidity ratio together. Follow AnyHVAC for practical checks; optional newsletter on the website.
