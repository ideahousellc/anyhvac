2. Supply air is not outdoor air

Purpose: Build trust around room-air-change inputs and send appropriate design questions to the existing Air Distribution Tools.

Destination: https://www.anyhvac.net/tools/air-distribution

William’s primary metric: Instagram saves per reached account from native Insights; report absolute saves and reach too. LinkedIn engagement and destination referrals are secondary, where available.

Engineering boundary: Illustrative inputs, not a prescribed ventilation rate or measured building. Outdoor and supply example flows use the same reference basis. Nominal ACH does not establish outdoor-air delivery, mixing effectiveness or code compliance. Diagram is a simplified mixing concept, not a specific unit layout.

LINKEDIN — exact caption
Supply airflow and outdoor airflow answer different questions.

A room receiving 500 CFM in 5,000 ft³ has a nominal supply-air rate of 6 ACH. If only 100 CFM of that flow is verified outdoor air on the same reference basis, its nominal outdoor-air rate is 1.2 ACH.

These are illustrative calculations, not ventilation recommendations. ACH alone does not establish outdoor-air delivery, mixing effectiveness or code compliance.

4. Dew point and wet bulb answer different questions

Purpose: Teach a practical air-property distinction, demonstrate engineering care and lead qualified users to the existing psychrometric calculator.

Destination: https://www.anyhvac.net/tools/psychrometric-calculator

William’s primary metric: Instagram saves per reached account from native Insights; report absolute saves and reach. LinkedIn engagement and qualified psychrometric page referrals are secondary where available.

Engineering boundary: Conceptual explanations, not a field measurement procedure or measured air state. Condensation depends on surface conditions; no diagnostic conclusion follows from an illustrative pipe. The normal unsaturated-state ordering is dew point ≤ wet bulb ≤ dry bulb; values coincide at saturation. Use the correct input mode and project pressure.

LINKEDIN — exact caption
Dew point and wet bulb are related, but they are not interchangeable inputs.

Dew point describes the saturation temperature reached by cooling without changing moisture content at fixed pressure. Wet bulb describes evaporative cooling and depends on temperature, moisture and pressure.

For normal unsaturated conditions: dew point ≤ wet bulb ≤ dry bulb. At saturation, the three coincide.

The six-slide explainer shows which question each property answers. In the free AnyHVAC Psychrometric Calculator, choose the matching moisture-input mode and set project elevation or atmospheric pressure.
