# Campaign #003 V2 — creative-director storyboard review

**Status:** STORYBOARD PROPOSAL ONLY — NOT APPROVED FOR PRODUCTION OR PUBLICATION  
**Owner decision requested:** **APPROVE STORYBOARD / REVISE STORYBOARD / REJECT STORYBOARD**  
**Proposed format:** 1080 × 1920, 30 fps, 26 seconds, music-led, no narration  
**Canonical destination:** https://www.anyhvac.net/tools/duct-calculator

## 1. One-sentence campaign concept

Ride with the air through a recognizable galvanized duct as the passage physically
narrows, feel velocity and wall interaction intensify, then follow the duct's round
collar directly into the real AnyHVAC Duct Calculator to evaluate the design point.

## 2. Why V1 failed creatively

V1 described a duct without making the viewer feel inside one. Its rounded rectangle,
grid, light streaks, split comparison, text-dominant layouts, information banners, and
full-page calculator capture read as animated engineering information. The frames had
motion, but they did not have a believable place, physical camera, material detail, or
visual discovery. Remove V1's copy and the story largely disappears.

## 3. V2 creative philosophy

V2 is one continuous mechanical journey, not a sequence of slides. The HVAC story is
carried by changing geometry, parallax, metal surfaces, passing seams, moving air,
light, and camera behavior. Text only names what the viewer has just experienced.
Branding waits until the tool has earned its entrance.

The text-removal test passes: without copy, the viewer still enters a real-looking
duct, sees the walls converge, experiences increased apparent air speed and stronger
wall interaction, finds a round collar, and arrives at an operating duct calculator.

## 5. The first three seconds, frame by frame

**Frame 0:** The viewer is already inches above a galvanized duct floor, moving toward
a deep vanishing point. A reinforcement rib is half-entering from the top edge, a cold
work light rakes the right wall, and two dust motes streak past the lens. There is no
text and no logo.

**0.5 second:** The first rib has passed overhead, producing a strong parallax wipe.
The next seam is visible ahead, fine metal texture slides under the light, and airflow
filaments briefly catch the beam. The shot unmistakably reads as an HVAC duct interior.

**1 second:** Forward movement remains steady; `SAME AIRFLOW` is attached to the
upper-left wall plane. Repeat seams establish a baseline visual tempo while the far
opening remains wide.

**2 seconds:** The fabricated transition is underway. Side walls visibly angle inward,
the ceiling is closer, `LESS AREA` has appeared, and air traces begin lengthening. The
viewer can understand the change even if muted and even if the words are removed.

**3 seconds:** The camera punches into the smaller section on a music impact. Metal
walls now fill more of the peripheral frame, seams arrive faster, and longer filaments
surge downstream. `HIGHER VELOCITY` confirms the visual consequence.

**Why someone would not swipe:** Frame 0 withholds setup and begins with unusual physical
access—an impossible-to-enter but immediately recognizable HVAC environment. Within
three seconds, the environment transforms around the viewer, the apparent speed rises,
and the camera crosses a real mechanical threshold on a synchronized impact. Curiosity
comes from experiencing a consequence, not waiting for an explanation.

## 6. Visual environment

The duct interior is cool galvanized steel rather than a blue neon tunnel. Four wall
planes meet at folded longitudinal corners; transverse reinforcement ribs, Pittsburgh-
style seam cues, rivet/fastener details, subtle oil-canning, and restrained zinc-spangle
texture supply mechanical identity. One hard neutral work light creates moving specular
reflections; warmer seam glints are used only during the friction beat. The transition
from rectangular run to round collar is visibly fabricated from sheet metal, with no
impossible folding geometry. Air is represented by sparse illuminated filaments, lint,
and motes—never smoke, liquid, or a numerical flow field.

## 7. Camera language

- One motivated forward journey from duct interior to tool; cuts are hidden by ribs,
  wall passes, or matched geometry.
- Low off-center opening for tension, centered alignment for comprehension, macro wall
  skim for texture, then a controlled roll into the circular calculator.
- Wide-lens perspective and near-object parallax create speed; speed is not conveyed by
  shaking or blur alone.
- Calculator coverage uses macro product cinematography: crop, focus, short tracks, and
  readable holds. The full webpage is never the composition.
- Camera motion eases with physical inertia and lands on musical beats.

## 8. Motion language

Motion is directional, mechanical, and causal. Passing seams establish tempo; narrowing
geometry causes stronger parallax and longer air traces; wall proximity introduces
disturbance; the round collar supplies rotation; calculator controls respond to real
interaction. UI editorial highlights follow actual elements and never replace them.
Particles are deterministic and sparse enough to avoid a sci-fi energy-tunnel look.

## 9. Text strategy

The main story uses only seven short phrases: `SAME AIRFLOW`, `LESS AREA`, `HIGHER
VELOCITY`, `FRICTION CHANGES TOO`, `DUCT SIZE MATTERS.`, `EVALUATE THE DESIGN POINT`,
and `SIZE IT FASTER.` The product name and `FREE • NO SIGNUP` appear only after the
payoff. Copy lives on physical planes or beside real UI targets; there are no cards,
paragraphs, title screen, or explanatory banners. `CONCEPTUAL` is present but quiet.
Each major phrase is readable inside vertical-video safe zones and the film still tells
its story with every editorial phrase removed.

## 11. George's recommended transition

**A. Collar-to-wheel match.** It is the only option in which the HVAC object itself

## 12. Calculator reveal plan

Capture the actual local route after hydration at a viewport designed for editorial
cropping. Record or capture deterministic real states rather than animating fake values:

1. Begin from a valid alternate design state so the real change to `5,000 CFM` is
   visible.
2. Enter/select `5,000 CFM` through the actual input and capture the wheel response.
3. Select the actual `0.10 in.w.g./100 ft` quick control and capture the response.
4. Reframe the authentic results: `25.6 in` calculated round diameter, `26 in` suggested
   nominal, `24 in × 23 in` primary rectangular option, and the displayed real velocity.
5. Reverify every value in the final captured build before render; if the application
   output differs, the video follows the application rather than this proposal.

The capture excludes site navigation, introductory copy, ads, and unrelated page area.
The wheel, real inputs, actual selected state, and real result regions are each large
enough to read on a phone. Editorial camera moves and focus treatments are composited
around the capture, but no replacement calculator UI or invented response is allowed.

## 13. Music direction

**Style:** Modern electronic-mechanical: dry industrial percussion, restrained sub,
short tonal pulses, brushed-metal texture, and one memorable three-note synth figure.
No corporate chord wash, meditation bed, stock risers, periodic warning beeps, or
trailer bombast.

**Energy:** Immediate 7/10 start, 8/10 through the constriction, momentary 5/10 vacuum
before the calculator match, 8/10 product payoff, decisive short ending.

**Tempo:** Approximately **124 BPM**, 4/4. The 0.484-second quarter-note supports
sub-second visual beats without forcing frantic cutting.

**Rhythm:** Kick-led pulse; eighth-note mechanical ticks establish the full-size duct;
sixteenth subdivisions enter as the duct narrows; a brushed-metal counter-rhythm
supports the friction pass. Avoid a constant four-on-the-floor feel.

**Build and major beats:**

- 0:00.0: music and motion start together.
- 0:01.45: geometry begins changing on the bar pickup.
- 0:02.90: impact crossing into the smaller section.
- 0:05.32: metallic layer enters at the wall pass.
- 0:07.74: rib wipe and harmonic opening.
- 0:10.16: collar fills frame; percussion thins.
- 0:11.61: **calculator reveal** lands after a half-beat vacuum.
- 0:15.48: second build begins with friction interaction.
- 0:18.87: results payoff opens the stereo field.
- 0:21.77: product-name phrase.
- 0:23.71: CTA downbeat.
- 0:25.65: final kick; controlled tail ends at 0:26.0.

The existing deterministic local WAV generator can create this structure and preserve
clear commercial provenance. It must be rearranged from V1: 124 BPM timing, a real
break before the reveal, more varied mechanical timbres, the three-note motif, and a
shorter CTA cadence. It is sufficient for a locally authored final track, although an
early listening pass must reject test-tone timbres or obvious short-loop repetition.
No music is generated in this storyboard phase.

## 14. SFX direction

SFX support the track instead of replacing it: moving air throughout; close rib passes;
subtle metallic seam ticks; constriction whoosh; one dry wall-skimming texture; collar
rotation; authentic key/tap/click cues; two soft result impacts. Air loudness may rise
with the perceived experience but is not calibrated data. No alarms, gauge beeps,
impacts on every text entrance, exaggerated turbulence, or cinematic explosion. SFX
can be synthesized locally and recorded as original/internal media; no SFX is generated
in this phase.

## 16. What can be produced entirely locally

The complete recommended concept can be produced locally with the installed Remotion,
React, browser, CSS/SVG, and available canvas support. The repository already has a
deterministic render pipeline, original audio synthesis, authentic hydrated screenshot
capture, the real calculator, a circular product form suited to the match, brand logos,
and a ductwork reference image. A deterministic Chrome DevTools capture script can be
added later to drive actual inputs and save state frames without introducing a major
dependency. Remotion can composite those captures, generate the pseudo-3D tunnel,
animate camera and particles, and mix locally authored music/SFX.

Three.js/WebGL is not currently a direct project dependency. It is unnecessary for the
recommended V2; adding it now would increase production and render risk without first
proving that perspective planes and masks are insufficient.

## 17. External asset that could materially improve quality

No external asset is required. The one low-risk enhancement worth testing after
storyboard approval is a **seamless galvanized-metal PBR/texture set** (base color and,
if useful, roughness/normal information) from a demonstrably CC0 or public-domain
library. Its role is to keep close wall passes from looking like flat gradients. It
must permit commercial use, modification, and social distribution; provenance must
record the source URL, author/provider, exact license, acquisition date, attribution
requirement, and original file.

An external AI video plate is not recommended by default. If local previsualization
fails, the exact request would be a vertical, loopable, photoreal interior of clean
galvanized rectangular HVAC duct moving through a fabricated narrowing transition into
a round collar, with no text, people, branding, smoke, unsafe condition, or impossible
geometry. Any provider would need explicit commercial-output rights, reproducibility
and provenance acceptable for publication, and owner approval before generation or
purchase. No account, API, asset search, download, or generation is authorized now.

## 18. Technical safeguards

- Preserve the conceptual relationship `Q = V × A`: the same represented volumetric
  airflow through less area produces higher average velocity.
- Do not visualize particle count as changing airflow quantity; reuse a stable seeded
  population and alter traversal speed/spacing only.
- Treat friction as an implication under comparable duct conditions, not as a universal
  target or a standalone prediction of total pressure/system performance.
- Keep `CONCEPTUAL` visible during airflow visualization and use the qualifier `SAME
  AIRFLOW • COMPARABLE DUCT CONDITIONS` for the friction beat.
- Do not show a universal velocity limit, friction target, required duct size, design
  approval, energy savings, noise prediction, or total-system conclusion.
- Use only valid states from the actual calculator. Drive the real DOM, capture after
  hydration, and reverify all displayed results against the final local build.
- Preserve the application logic unchanged; production may capture it but may not alter
  formulas or fabricate responses.
- Use believable sheet-metal transitions and airflow direction. Avoid smoke, liquid
  flow, reverse-moving particles, impossible seams, or unsafe field-work imagery.
- Label captured inputs as an example design point, not a recommendation.
- Keep all text, real UI targets, and CTA inside 9:16 platform-safe regions and verify
  at phone size before final rendering.
- Record provenance and commercial rights for every non-repository visual or audio
  asset before it can pass the publication quality gate.

## 19. Expected final duration

**26.0 seconds.** The duct story earns 12.4 seconds, real calculator interaction and
results earn 9.4 seconds, and product identification/CTA use the final 4.2 seconds.

## 20. Biggest creative risk

The friction beat could accidentally imply that bright wall effects are a literal or
quantified view of friction. Restraint, near-wall tracer behavior, the comparable-
conditions qualifier, and the absence of invented gauges are essential. If the beat
still reads as heat or danger during phone-size previsualization, remove the warm color
and rely on texture, near-wall disturbance, and sound.

## 21. Biggest technical risk

The procedural tunnel may still look like a flat animated box if material texture,
occlusion, seam continuity, lighting, and near-object parallax are not convincing. The
first production gate should therefore be a silent 0–3 second low-resolution
previsualization reviewed at phone size before calculator capture or audio production.
Failure at that gate triggers the reference-line transition fallback or a separately
approved external material/plate evaluation; it does not justify rendering the full
video.
