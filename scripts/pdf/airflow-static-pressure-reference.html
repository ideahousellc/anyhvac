Pressure terms

Static pressure

Pressure in the air system apart from the kinetic contribution represented by velocity pressure.

Velocity pressure

Pressure associated with air motion; pitot velocity pressure is total minus static pressure.

Total pressure

Static pressure plus velocity pressure at the same point.

Pressure drop

Pressure difference across a component or section at a stated operating condition.

TESP

Total external static pressure across the equipment's defined external boundary.

Available static

Design pressure budget left for distribution losses after applicable external component losses.

Safe and responsible use

Follow current manufacturer procedures. De-energize equipment before creating test ports. Confirm locations are clear of coils, wiring, tubing, controls, and rotating components. Restore ports after use. Document operating mode, fan setting, filter, coil, damper, and accessory condition.

TESP is not airflow. Use the correct manufacturer fan data and known operating conditions.

Measured TESP is not design available static pressure. Do not enter it directly as Fan External Static Pressure without a valid design workflow.

TESP measurement workflow

  1. Identify exact equipment and obtain current manufacturer instructions.
  2. Define which components are inside or outside the rated boundary.
  3. Choose only procedure-approved return and supply test locations.
  4. Zero the manometer and connect the static probe correctly.
  5. Run the required stable mode and airflow setting.
  6. Record both readings and material operating conditions.
  7. Compare the magnitude with the correct manufacturer data.

Record with every reading

  • Equipment model
  • Operating mode
  • Fan or airflow setting
  • Probe locations
  • Filter condition
  • Coil condition
  • Damper positions
  • Installed accessories
  • Instrument and range
  • Calibration status
  • Observed instability
  • Date and technician

Core relationships

Airflow

CFM = FPM x ft2

Velocity

FPM = CFM / ft2

Rectangular area

A = W x H

Round area

A = pi x D2 / 4

Use actual internal dimensions. Internal liner reduces free area. Convert inches to feet before calculating ft2.

Conceptual multi-point traverse

Use the governing traverse method and point locations. Record each point. For pitot work, convert each velocity-pressure reading to velocity before averaging velocities. Then multiply average velocity by actual area.

Capture hood checklist

  1. Confirm hood size, range, and flow direction.
  2. Cover the complete outlet and establish a consistent seal.
  3. Allow readings to stabilize.
  4. Repeat as required by the procedure.
  5. Use only validated corrections or K-factors.
  6. Document terminal and test conditions.

A hood adds resistance and may alter flow. One terminal reading does not establish total system airflow.

Common measurement errors

  • Wrong TESP boundary
  • Instrument not zeroed
  • TESP called airflow
  • Damaged or leaking tubing
  • External duct dimensions
  • Internal liner ignored
  • One center reading
  • Poor traverse plane
  • Misaligned velocity probe
  • Pressure averaged before velocity
  • Generic grille free area
  • Generic hood correction

Instrument context

Hot wireLocal/low velocity; sensitive to direction, contamination, and turbulence.
VaneFace or larger-opening velocity; watch angle, coverage, and grille effects.
